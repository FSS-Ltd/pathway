# Ireland national checklist pack (2.05)

Reviewed 2026-10-18. Catalogue 1.6.0 publishes a **partial national core** for six institutional sectors and eight topics. The machine-readable actions and 26 official/provider source records are in `apps/api/src/compliance/catalogue/ireland/`. Items record their source class, applicability, expected evidence and review basis. Checklist completion is not legal certification.

Ireland has distinct routes for recognised schools, non-recognised schools, early-years services and school-age childcare. [Department of Education guidance](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/policy-information/home-schooling-or-attendance-at-non-recognised-schools/) identifies the child's **parent** as responsible for Tusla registration when a child attends a non-recognised school; the checklist presents provider communication as a recommendation. [Tusla school-age](https://www.tusla.ie/services/family-community-support/school-age-services/i-am-a-school-age-provider/regulations-and-legislation/) and [early-years](https://www.tusla.ie/services/preschool-services/new-providers/) routes depend on actual care, ages and exemptions, not the selected sector or ACE branding.

[Children First Act 2015 section 11](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2015/act/36/section/11/enacted/en/html) requires a relevant-service provider's child risk assessment and Child Safeguarding Statement, with review at intervals no longer than 24 months. The portal reminder is set earlier. [Recognised-school procedures](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/policy-information/child-protection-procedures-in-schools/) have a 2025 edition that recognised schools must formally adopt by 31 December 2026; the 2023 edition remains until adoption. [National Vetting Bureau Act section 12](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2012/act/47/section/12/enacted/en/html) requires a disclosure before a relevant organisation permits relevant work. Role duties and statutory exceptions must be assessed; an adult portal account alone does not trigger vetting.

The pack uses [EU GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) and [Irish Data Protection Commission school guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/data-protection-toolkit-schools), not UK GDPR, for privacy. [HSA safety-statement guidance](https://www.hsa.ie/topics/managing_health_and_safety/safety_statement_and_risk_assessment/), [Irish fire guidance](https://www.fireireland.ie/fire-safety/business-fire-safety/legislation/), [FSAI food registration](https://www.fsai.ie/business-advice/starting-a-food-business/register), Tusla care standards and actual ICCE provider terms supply the other national actions.

Every Irish sector/topic cell remains `PARTIALLY_PUBLISHED` and its regional status `IN_REVIEW`. School recognition, Tusla service classification, local-authority or fire conditions, specialist permissions, childcare staffing ratios, additional educational needs, individual vetting eligibility, detailed data-rights/DPIA/DPO duties, transport and boarding require further review. The item-level N/A policy requires a documented reason where an exception is allowed. A missing profile fact remains unresolved; it must not fall back to England or silently count as no obligation.

Scripted source checks returned HTTP 200 for the Irish Statute Book, Tusla, regulator, FSAI and provider pages. Some gov.ie pages reject scripted requests, while EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202; the content and citation paths were cross-checked with official source search. These access limitations do not change the remaining partial-coverage status. See [PROGRESS.md](../../PROGRESS.md) for test and PR evidence.
