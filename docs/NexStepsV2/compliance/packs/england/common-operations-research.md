# England common operational baseline: RIDDOR and equality

Research checked: 15 October 2026. Scope: current England 2.01 only; implementation follows the research below. These are common baseline omissions, not merely specialist/local gaps. Adding them would not establish exhaustive legal coverage or certification. Tax, payroll and finance are excluded.

## Recommended catalogue shape

Add two unconditional **RECOMMENDATION** assessment-and-fulfil rows across all institutional sectors. Their assessed underlying duties are statutory, but current profile facts cannot reliably determine every responsible-person/service/association route. Keep assessment unconditional rather than inventing a universal reporting duty or inferring legal status from product type. Evidence of why a route does not apply can complete an assessment; it must not exempt duties that actually apply. No automatic expiry or universal annual review date is supported.

1. Identify responsibility for incident reporting; assess incidents against RIDDOR criteria, make required notifications/reports and keep required records.
2. Identify the applicable equality route for each service, education and membership activity; prevent unlawful discrimination and implement reasonable adjustments under that route.

Existing school accessibility and EYFS regulator incident-notification rows remain separate: they do not replace these assessments. Checklist signals may show incident/policy/accessibility evidence only; they cannot infer legal reportability or automatically attest compliance.

## RIDDOR

### Primary sources and verified propositions

- [RIDDOR 2013 regulation 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1471/regulation/1): principal commencement **1 October 2013**; regulation 18(2) has its own immediate-following provision. Current consolidated text was checked, not assumed to be identical to the original instrument.
- [Regulation 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1471/regulation/3): the responsible person depends on the event and undertaking. Generally the employer handles employee events; for relevant non-worker/self-employed events, the person controlling the premises in connection with an undertaking may be responsible. Specialist categories have other rules. Therefore `employsStaff=false` does not establish exemption; a generic premises-control flag also cannot settle the statutory test.
- [Regulation 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1471/regulation/4): work-related specified injuries and more than seven consecutive days' incapacity for routine work are reportable, subject to exclusions. Exclude the accident day when counting; the latter report is due as soon as practicable and within 15 days. It is not a seven-working-day threshold.
- [Regulation 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1471/regulation/5): a non-worker's work-related accident leading to transfer from the accident site to hospital for injury treatment can be reportable; there is also a separate hospital-premises limb. A child attending hospital does not by itself prove RIDDOR applies. Check work connection, treatment and exclusions.
- [Regulation 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1471/regulation/12): retain required event/diagnosis records and records of work accidents causing more than three consecutive days' routine-work incapacity, excluding accident day. The latter recording threshold differs from the reporting threshold. Retention is at least **three years from entry**, not three years from incident. Existing records can satisfy the duty where they contain required particulars.
- [HSE reportable incidents](https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/reportable-incidents.htm): assessment also covers work-related deaths, listed dangerous occurrences, qualifying occupational diseases and specified exposure events, with applicable exceptions. Use linked detailed criteria rather than making every near miss or infection reportable. Issuer: HSE; guidance, not the legislation itself. Page updated 16 March 2023; original publication date unverified.
- [HSE reporting timing](https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/when-do-i-report.htm): most reportable deaths, specified injuries, non-worker hospital-treatment accidents and dangerous occurrences require notification without delay and a report within ten days. Over-seven-day incapacity uses the fifteen-day route; report qualifying occupational disease upon diagnosis. Issuer: HSE; guidance; updated 11 February 2025, original publication date unverified.

### Action, applicability and evidence

Suggested action: **Assess RIDDOR responsibility and reportability; make required reports promptly and retain the required records.**

Applies as an assessment to schools, learning centres, childcare providers, churches, charities and clubs. Actual statutory reporting depends on the responsible-person definition, work connection, event category and exclusions. Voluntary or charitable status alone is not an exemption. Do not describe all pupil playground injuries, all absences, every hospital visit or ordinary public-road collisions as automatically reportable. Other regulator/safeguarding reporting can coexist.

Expected evidence: identified responsible person and escalation route; classification of actual incidents with reasons; report acknowledgement/reference and submission date where required; required incident-record particulars and retention arrangement. Where no qualifying incident occurred, readiness/procedure evidence supports the assessment, not a fictitious report. Minimise sensitive medical detail in tracker notes and use protected links to incident records.

Specialist residuals: exact disease/exposure lists, transport exceptions, construction/dangerous-occurrence details and unusual employment/premises relationships require case-specific application. The assessment must link current detailed criteria and preserve these limits.

## Equality: services, education and associations

### Primary sources and verified propositions

- [Equality Act 2010 section 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/20): where an applicable Part imposes the duty, reasonable adjustments address substantial disadvantage from practices, physical features and lack of auxiliary aids/services, as modified by the relevant Schedule. Accessible information can be required. Do not charge a disabled person for a required adjustment, subject to express statutory exceptions.
- [Section 29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/29): public-facing services, including free services, and relevant public functions have discrimination and adjustment duties. Paid/free, charity/faith and state/private labels do not resolve coverage. Protected-characteristic and harassment provisions are not identical across routes.
- [Section 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/28): Part 3 does not duplicate conduct covered by premises, work or education Parts, including conduct excepted there. Its age protection excludes under-18s and it excludes marriage/civil partnership. Avoid stating every protected characteristic applies identically to every activity.
- [Section 85](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/85): school responsible bodies have the school admissions, education, benefits and exclusion duties, including adjustments. Determine actual school status and responsible body; ACE affiliation does not substitute for this classification.
- [Schedule 13 paragraphs 2–3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/schedule/13): school-pupil adjustments require the practice and auxiliary-aid branches, **not the physical-feature branch**. Keep school accessibility-planning duties in their existing dedicated row. Further/higher education has a different adjustment scope. A school providing a separate public service may require separate route analysis.
- [Section 107](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/107) and [section 101](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/101): Part 7 associations generally have at least 25 members and rules regulating admission with selection. Profit and incorporation are not decisive. Do not infer that every club is this kind of association, or that a smaller club's public-facing services are exempt from Part 3. Section 107 brings Schedule 15 adjustment provisions into effect; detailed guest/member situations must use that route.
- [EHRC services, public functions and associations Code, chapter 7](https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/codes-practice/code-practice-services-public-functions-and-associations/chapter-seven): the duty is anticipatory and continuing, directed to disabled people generally within the relevant population, rather than only responding to known requests. Reasonableness is contextual; there is no universal adjustment list or fixed review interval. Record decisions and review barriers as practice, users and circumstances change. Issuer: Equality and Human Rights Commission; statutory Code guidance, not a new standalone duty. Web page explicitly published and updated **5 August 2026**; this is the page publication date, not commencement of the Act or a claim that every provision commenced then.

### Action, applicability and evidence

Suggested action: **Assess the equality duties for your actual services, education and membership activities; remove unlawful barriers and make the reasonable adjustments required by the applicable route.**

Use an unconditional recommendation assessment for all institutional sectors. Actual duties follow activities/legal category, with any lawful exceptions examined specifically. Do not presume a blanket religious exemption, universal association status, government funding or universal public-sector equality duty. For schools, preserve the narrower physical-feature adjustment distinction and existing accessibility-plan row. For non-school learning centres and childcare services, identify the actual service/education route rather than inheriting school status from reused screens.

Expected evidence: route and responsible-body assessment; accessible admissions/service information and adjustment process; barriers identified, decisions/reasons, implemented aids or alternative arrangements; relevant staff instructions; review when circumstances change. These are expected evidence of fulfilment, not a universally prescribed statutory equality register. Do not collect unnecessary disability diagnoses in checklist notes. An absence of current disabled users does not by itself justify doing nothing under an anticipatory duty.

Remaining specialist scope: employment adjustments (different Part and Schedule), particular charity/religion/single-sex exceptions, further/higher education and qualification bodies, physical alterations and landlord/consent constraints, and fact-sensitive reasonableness disputes. General assessment should route these for advice rather than asserting a universal exception or universal building alteration requirement.

Do not use Royal Assent, made dates or web updates as a universal legal effective date.

These findings support two additional common assessments while retaining partial coverage and visible specialist/site-specific gaps. No database or external-state changes were made.
