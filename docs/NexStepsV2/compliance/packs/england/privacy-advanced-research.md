# England 2.01: advanced privacy source closure

Verified/currentAsOf: **2026-10-15**. Research supporting the bounded advanced privacy assessment catalogue. Scope: England institutional operations, with UK-wide data-protection/PECR rules applied to the actual controller/activity. This is not EU GDPR equivalence research.

## Verification and dates

Read official legislation.gov.uk XML for the provisions linked below, including the amending provisions where inserted GDPR articles are awkward to retrieve directly; cross-checked current ICO guidance and DfE's June 2026 biometric publication. Statutory duties derive from legislation; ICO interpretation and recommended evidence are identified separately. No third-party summaries are relied on.

[SI 2026/82 regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/regulation/2/data.xml) brings the relevant DUAA changes into force **5 February 2026**, so far as not already commenced: section 80/Sch6 automated decisions; section81 children's design protection; section85/Sch7 transfers; section112/Sch12 storage/access; section114 charity electronic marketing. [Government commencement status](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-plans-for-commencement), updated12 October2026, corroborates staged commencement. Royal assent is not the effective date for all provisions.

For unchanged UK GDPR Articles24/26/28 and pre-existing PECR provisions, record currentAsOf2026-10-15 and effectiveFrom=null with reason: this review verified current consolidated provisions, not every original/transitional commencement. Do not attach5February2026 to unchanged duties. New/changed provisions below have exact commencement evidence. These dates describe research, not a prescribed annual review interval.

## Recommended publication approach

Add bounded, understandable controller-accountability actions rather than dozens of speculative profile flags. An action can require the controller to identify its systems, processing, recipients and applicable legal routes, and keep evidence of the result. It must not claim that merely completing this assessment fulfils every resulting operational duty.

Suggested common action: **“Review the site's personal-data systems, recipients and higher-risk uses; record the controls or follow-up actions required for each.”** Applicability: UK GDPR controller, across all six institutional sectors. Authority: UK GDPR [Article24](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/24/data.xml), statutory duty to implement, demonstrate and review appropriate measures. Evidence: dated proportionate inventory, responsible owner, processing purposes, actual roles/recipients, conditional assessments below, unresolved questions and remediation. An inventory is a practical accountability method, not a statutory requirement to buy a particular tool or appoint a DPO. Keep Article30's separate scope/exceptions; do not turn this into an unqualified full-ROPA mandate.

Where facts are unknown, the implementation duty remains unresolved, not automatically inapplicable. Evidence/notes can contain an assessed “not used” outcome with rationale. A catalogue row applicable to all controllers should be worded as assessment plus necessary implementation, not “obtain consent for all processing”. Completion requires the scoped work to be done, not just discovering unfinished controls. Do not erase remaining coverage gaps or call this legal certification.

## 1. Processor and joint-controller arrangements

**Proposed action:** “Identify each recipient's actual role and put the required written arrangements and safeguards in place before sharing or outsourcing personal data.”

Authority/provisions: UK GDPR [Article28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/28/data.xml), especially1–4,9–10, and [Article26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/26/data.xml). Classification: statutory duties; practical inventory/review method is implementation guidance. Scope: all institutional sectors when relevant relationships exist; currentAsOf2026-10-15, original commencement not re-established here.

For processors, check adequate guarantees and a binding written contract/legal act covering processing scope, documented instructions including transfers, confidentiality, security, subprocessor approval/change notices, rights/breach/DPIA assistance, return/deletion and audit information. Actual purposes/means determine role; a supplier's “processor” label is insufficient. If both parties jointly determine purposes/means, transparently allocate responsibilities including notices and rights and make the essence of the arrangement available to affected people. They retain rights against either joint controller. Do not demand Article28 contracts for every independent-controller disclosure or infer joint control from a data-sharing relationship alone.

Evidence: supplier/recipient role assessment, signed terms and schedules, due diligence, approved subprocessors/change process, joint-controller arrangement and accessible summary where applicable. Site-specific unknowns: actual processing, instructions, vendors and contractual responsibilities. Those are local facts, not missing national legal research.

## 2. International transfers

**Proposed action:** “Map overseas recipients and access, identify who initiates each restricted transfer, and document the applicable transfer route and safeguards.”

Authority: DUAA [Schedule7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/schedule/7/data.xml), inserting Articles44A/45A–C and amending46/49; statutory, amended regime effective5February2026. Article44A requires an approved-regulations route, appropriate safeguards or a specific-situation derogation, alongside other GDPR obligations. Under Article46(1A),(6),(7), safeguard users must reasonably and proportionately consider the data-protection test met: protection after transfer must not be materially lower. The nature/volume and circumstances matter. A signed generic processor agreement alone is not a transfer safeguard assessment.

ICO [restricted-transfer guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/a-guide-to-international-transfers/are-we-making-a-restricted-transfer/) applies a three-part assessment: UK GDPR processing, initiating transfer to an organisation outside UK, and a separate legal entity. Remote access can count; own employees in the same entity differ from an overseas contractor. Hosting geography alone does not answer the test. Determine the controller/processor initiating the relevant transfer rather than treating every supplier chain transfer as directly initiated by the school.

Evidence: recipient legal entities/countries and access map; responsibility assessment; currently valid adequacy/approval scope or appropriate instrument plus documented test and necessary supplementary controls; justified derogation if applicable; review on material changes. Do not hardcode “EU hosted = no transfer” or presume a country's approval covers every recipient. Destination-specific adequacy/instrument checks require actual destinations; this research does not certify any supplier or destination.

## 3. Children's biometrics in education

**Proposed conditional action:** “Before automated biometric recognition of under-18 pupils/students, complete the education-specific notification/consent process, honour objections, and offer reasonable alternatives.”

Authority: Protection of Freedoms Act2012 [s26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/9/section/26/data.xml), [s27](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/9/section/27/data.xml), [s28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/9/section/28/data.xml); statutory. Exact commencement **1September2013**, [SI2013/2104 article2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2104/article/2/data.xml). DfE [current England guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/protection-of-biometric-information-of-children-in-schools), updated17June2026, supports operational interpretation.

Trigger: processing by/on behalf of the relevant authority of a school,16–19 Academy or further-education institution, with biometric information intended for automated biometric recognition, child under18. Legal institution status matters, not an ACE/product screen. This special regime is not automatically imposed on every church/nursery/club or every photograph; GDPR obligations remain separately applicable.

Notify each parent before first processing, subject to exact s27 exceptions; obtain written consent from at least one and ensure no parental objection/withdrawal. A child's own refusal or objection prevents processing even with parental consent. Provide reasonable alternative access to the service. Record relevant s27 exceptions/substitute care arrangements under s28 rather than inventing a blanket parental-notification exemption. These duties are additional to GDPR lawful-basis/special-category/security duties, not satisfied by an age13 online-consent rule.

Evidence: system/age/institution classification; notification and written-consent records; objections/withdrawal stop process; documented statutory exceptions; alternative access; GDPR assessment. Local uncertainties are whether the system exists and satisfies the statutory definition, pupil ages, actual legal institution and consent circumstances.

## 4. PECR electronic marketing and storage/access

**Proposed assessment/action:** “Review marketing channels and website/app storage or access; implement the applicable consent, exception and objection controls.” PECR can apply to terminal information even when it is not personal data; do not describe it as GDPR-only.

Electronic-mail authority: [PECR22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22/data.xml), [23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/23/data.xml), statutory. Unsolicited electronic marketing to individual subscribers needs current consent unless an exact soft-opt-in route applies. Products/services route requires directly obtained details in sale/negotiation, own similar offerings and collection/each-message opt-out. Sender identity and a valid cessation address are required under23. Corporate subscribers differ; UK GDPR rights remain relevant. Determine whether a communication is actually marketing rather than presume every administrative school message is promotional. This electronic-mail review does not purport to cover separate live/automated marketing-call rules.

Charity route: [DUAA114](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/114/data.xml) inserts22(3A),(5), effective5February2026. Actual legal charity status is required, not selected sector. Own charitable-purpose marketing, qualifying directly obtained supporter/interest details, and collection/each-message opt-outs are necessary. ICO's [current detailed guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/) limits reliance to qualifying details obtained on/after5February2026; old lists cannot simply be grandfathered. Legitimate interests does not replace a PECR consent requirement.

Storage/access authority: [PECR6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/6/data.xml) and [ScheduleA1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/schedule/A1/data.xml), amended effective5February2026. Obtain informed consent unless the exact communication, strictly necessary, statistical, appearance/functionality or emergency-assistance exception applies. Statistical use must be solely service/site-improvement statistics with restricted sharing, clear information and free simple objection honoured; it excludes automatically emitted information from that exception. Appearance/functionality also requires clear information and objection. Do not say “all analytics exempt” or “all cookies always require consent”. ICO [final guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/) was updated29April2026, not a pending consultation.

Evidence: campaign classification, subscriber/contact provenance/date, lawful route, consent/suppression logs, unsubscribe tests; live technology inventory including third-party tags, purpose-by-purpose exception assessment, notice/control screenshots and tests. These facts cannot be inferred from charitable status or a website URL alone.

## 5. Children's online services and design

**Proposed action:** “Assess whether services likely accessed by under-18s are in scope, and document child-appropriate design controls where required.”

Authority: [DUAA81](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/81/data.xml), inserting GDPR25(1A),(1B),(4),(5), statutory, effective5February2026. For relevant information-society services likely accessed by children, assessment of appropriate measures must take account of protection/support, children's reduced awareness of processing risks/rights and varying developmental needs. Preventive/counselling services are excluded from this article's ISS definition; ordinary Article25 duties remain.

ICO [Children's Code introduction](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/introduction-to-the-childrens-code) distinguishes education processing generally from in-scope online education services normally remunerated, provided electronically at distance on individual request. Do not blanket-exempt every “school” or apply the code to every child record. Likely access, not solely intended child audience, matters. Review a vendor's independent-controller uses as well as the institution's own service.

The [code's legal status](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/about-this-code/) is a DPA123 statutory code considered under127, effective2September2020. Classify as statutory guidance/code, with underlying GDPR duties separate; do not portray every recommended implementation detail as an independent statutory offence.

Evidence: service/business-model/audience assessment, ages and access likelihood, privacy defaults and age-appropriate transparency, design-risk assessment and controls, supplier review. A DPIA is required only where its legal trigger is met; this assessment does not impose one universally.

## 6. Automated significant decisions

**Proposed action:** “Identify decisions without meaningful human involvement, assess their effect and data, and implement the applicable restrictions and challenge safeguards.”

Authority: [DUAA80](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/80/data.xml), UK GDPR22A–D; statutory, effective5February2026. Article22A defines solely automated as no meaningful human involvement and significant as legal/similarly significant effect. A ceremonial human approval is not enough. Article22B restricts special-category decisions to its precise exceptions and prohibits relevant solely automated significant decisions relying entirely/partly on Article6(1)(ea). Article22C requires information, representations, human intervention and contest mechanisms. Do not repeat the old blanket Article22 prohibition as the current general rule; other GDPR duties remain.

[SI2026/82 regulation5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/regulation/5/data.xml) preserves the previous regime for specified pre5February2026 decisions. Do not retrospectively rewrite historical decision assessments. Article22D enables supplementary regulations: this review found no specific additional instrument in primary-source search, but does not certify exhaustive absence; refresh before deploying a significant automated-decision feature.

Evidence: decision inventory including admissions/staffing or eligibility where actually used; impact/special-category/lawful-basis assessment; meaningful-human-review design; notices and tested challenge route; date/history. Ordinary reminders or a manual compliance checkbox are not automatically significant automated decisions. Supplier “AI” branding does not determine the statutory test.

## Closure and limits

The national routes above are researched sufficiently to draft bounded accountability/assessment actions with conditional implementation instructions. Missing local facts must remain explicit in evidence and follow-up, not be mislabelled unresearched national law or silently treated as “no duty”. No extra profile flags are requested in this handoff. The two PECR assessment rows apply regardless of GDPR controller status because sending/instigating marketing and terminal storage/access have their own statutory scope; the transfer assessment also applies to controllers and processors because either may initiate a restricted transfer. The other four assessments use the existing controller fact.

This does not close every privacy topic: record-specific education retention/access, separate telephone marketing, destination-specific transfer approvals, and service-specific regulatory questions are outside the verified detail here. Keep the England pack's remaining coverage statement accurate. No universal DPO, DPIA, fee-payment, biometric use, marketing or overseas-transfer duty is proposed. Focused catalogue tests verify source metadata and assessment applicability; no production data was accessed. The step delivery record contains final verification results.
